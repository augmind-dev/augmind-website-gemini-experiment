# When someone asks AI for a business like yours, does it name you?

Canonical page: https://www.augmind.ai/ai-search

Search used to rank you. An AI assistant either names you or it doesn't. This page explains how it decides, and how you can tell.

## Search ranked you. AI either names you or it doesn't.

| | Search results page | AI answer |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | About ten links | One written answer that names a few businesses |
| Stability | Fairly stable day to day | Changes each time the question is asked |
| How you check | Look up your position | Only as a rate, across many asks |
| What you win | A click | Being named, and sometimes linked |
| The entry ticket | Crawlable, indexed pages | The same: ordinary search work still comes first |

Fewer than 1 in 100 is the chance an AI assistant gives the same list of brands twice. A rate across repeated asks is stable enough to measure. A single answer is an anecdote. Source: SparkToro and Gumshoe, about 2,961 runs of 12 prompts, January 2026, reported by Search Engine Land. Not peer reviewed.

## Five steps. Five places to drop out.

1. Rewrite: none of your pages answer the searches it writes.
2. Search: your site isn't in the results it gets back.
3. Read: a firewall or robots.txt rule blocks its crawler.
4. Write: no passage on your site states clearly what you do, where, and for whom.
5. Cite: you're named but not linked, or a competitor is linked instead.

For local questions, ChatGPT may also use an approximate location.

## Each business is counted against its local competitors. The range shows what is real.

Levarum asks AI the questions your customers ask (25 questions, each asked 3 times), then says in plain numbers how often each business is named. The range sits behind every number and decides who is ahead. Levarum measures one AI engine today: OpenAI's model with web search.

Sample report (all businesses and figures are invented). Larkmoor Coffee, Ostley Falls. AI names Larkmoor Coffee in about 6 of every 10 answers; Bramblewick Roasters in about 5 in 10 (too close to call); Tindle & Rye Roasters in about 3 in 10 (behind Larkmoor). Larkmoor's website is linked in about 2 in 10 answers.

The counts: Larkmoor Coffee 44 of 75 (expect 33 to 55 if asked again); Bramblewick Roasters 36 of 75 (24 to 48); Tindle & Rye Roasters 19 of 75 (9 to 29). Website linked: 17 of 75 (7 to 27). When two ranges overlap, the report says "too close to call" rather than ranking them. The three asks of one question are not fully independent, so each range is an estimate.

## Being named and being linked are different results.

- Named: the business is named in the answer text. This feeds how often you are mentioned, compared with other local businesses.
- Linked: the answer links to the business's own site. Links to directories or review sites count as sources, not as links to you.

We report both and never blend them.

## Each AI leans on different sites.

For local businesses, Google's AI answers lean on Google's own profiles. ChatGPT draws on a wider mix of websites and directories.

| AI assistant | Cited most for local businesses | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews and AI Mode | Google Business Profile is the most-cited source. Then Yelp and Facebook. | Claim and complete your Google Business Profile, plus current Facebook and Yelp pages. |
| ChatGPT | Yelp is the directory BrightLocal found most often in ChatGPT's local answers. Business websites and other directories appear too. Other studies find much less Yelp, so results vary by method. | Keep your own pages clear, and claim Yelp and the directories for your trade. |
| All three | Business websites are cited too. | A clear page for each service you sell. |

Source: BrightLocal's local AI visibility study (2026), which looked at the sources Google's AI Overviews, Google's AI Mode and ChatGPT cite for local businesses. Third-party research, not peer reviewed; other studies report different results.

## Claim the profiles AI reads about you.

Claiming makes your details accurate. It doesn't buy a place in the answer.

- Google Business Profile: the source Google's AI answers cite most for local businesses. (Third-party study)
- Yelp: the directory BrightLocal found most often in ChatGPT's local answers. Other studies differ. (Third-party study)
- ChatGPT Business Hub: new, in beta since 10 November 2026. Claim by phone, business email, website domain or Shopify. OpenAI reviews each claim. OpenAI says claiming doesn't guarantee more visibility or control over its recommendations, and that ChatGPT may use other sources too. (Official, OpenAI)
- Your trade's directories: industry directories can matter too, such as Zocdoc for dentists and Avvo for lawyers. (Our view)

Sources: BrightLocal, as above; OpenAI Help Center, "Businesses in ChatGPT", read 22 November 2026.

## Work the levers in order. Access before content.

1. Access: let the AI search crawlers in, firewall included.
2. Index: make sure the search engines have your pages.
3. Clear answers: direct, quotable answers, with facts you can source.
4. Identity: the same name, address and details everywhere.
5. Mentions: reviews, listings and coverage on other sites.
6. Measure: baseline, change, measure again.

Path A, from memory: named, with no link. It changes when a new model ships. Lasting coverage on other sites moves it.
Path B, from a live search: cited, with a link. It changes within days. Indexing, clear passages and freshness move it.

We record whether web search ran on every answer, so the two paths can be told apart. Answers without a search are left out of the scores.

## Where you start decides what to do first.

- Low, named in fewer than 2 of every 10 answers. Be findable: let AI crawlers in, claim your profiles, use the same name, address and phone everywhere.
- Climbing, named in 2 to 5 of every 10. Be where AI looks for you: get onto the sites your report shows AI citing, and give each service a clear page.
- High, named in more than 5 of every 10. Keep it: keep details current and reviews coming, measure every month, and act only on changes bigger than the normal swing.

The bands are AugMind's own guide, not an industry standard. Nobody controls what an AI says.

## A report is evidence from one run. Not a promise.

What we don't claim: what one person sees in the ChatGPT app; results for an engine we didn't ask; that a move inside the margin means anything; rankings, traffic, leads or revenue; that a suggested step caused a change.

Always safe to say: scores are estimates from a limited number of answers, collected at one time from one stated location. Each report names the engine, model, location and date behind it. Nobody can promise a place in an AI answer.
